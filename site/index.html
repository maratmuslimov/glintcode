Scannable codes that look designed.

Glint is five visual codes that share one 48-bit payload, one generator and one camera reader. It runs in the browser, in Swift and in Kotlin.

What this measures

    What it doesn't

    • These are not photos from phones. Frames come from a browser renderer with added damage, so real cameras, glare and print quality are not covered here.
    • Zero wrong reads here doesn't mean they can't happen. When a code is badly damaged, the reader can rely on as little as 16 check bits, so have your server reject IDs it doesn't know.
    • Percentages are for one frame. A live camera gets many frames per second, so a person usually sees better results, but we haven't measured by how much.
    • The table covers the TypeScript reader. The Swift and Kotlin readers are compared with it on the same frames in the docs.

    Before you print a thousand

    When to use a QR code instead.

    • Phone cameras don't know Glint. People need your app, your website or an App Clip. If they will scan with the stock camera, use a QR code or put a small one beside the Glint.
    • It holds 6 bytes. That is an ID for your server to look up, not a URL. QR codes can hold the whole link.
    • It isn't a standard. Version 1 is one project's format. The spec and the test data are public, but no other scanners read it yet.
